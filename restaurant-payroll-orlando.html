Tipped Wages Under Florida Law

Florida's minimum wage steps up every September 30 under the constitutional amendment voters passed in 2020, which means a restaurant's pay rates have a scheduled change baked into every fall. Here is where the numbers stand.

PeriodFlorida minimum wageTipped cash wageTip credit
Now, through September 29, 2026$14.00 per hour$10.98 per hour$3.02
From September 30, 2026$15.00 per hour$11.98 per hour$3.02

Two rules do the real work here. First, if a server's cash wage plus tips does not reach the full minimum for the hours worked, the employer makes up the difference, so tip records have to be accurate to the shift. Second, the September rate change has to actually land in payroll: a kitchen still paying August rates in October is accruing a wage violation one week at a time. We set the new rates in QuickBooks Payroll ahead of the change, on schedule. Figures per the published schedule summarized by Fisher Phillips.

Tip Allocation, Done Right Weekly

Tips are where restaurant payroll actually breaks. Pooled tips split across servers, bussers, and bartenders, credit card tips paid through payroll, cash tips reported by the employee, and all of it feeding tax withholding that has to be right per person, per week.

One Orlando restaurant came to us with exactly that problem: tip allocation errors compounding every week, and six months of bookkeeping behind on top of them. We corrected the tip allocation procedures, rebuilt the historical records, and automated a weekly payroll for their 15 employees that has run reliably since. Fixing the payroll also fixed the reporting: the owner saw accurate food-cost margins for the first time, because payroll and books were finally telling the same story.

Weekly Pay Runs Without the Weekly Fire Drill

A weekly cycle means fifty-two chances a year for payroll to eat a morning. We configure the run in QuickBooks Payroll so hours and tip amounts go in, and everything downstream happens on its own: withholding calculated, direct deposits out, and the run posted to the general ledger with wages, taxes, and liabilities in the right accounts. No re-keying into a second system, no reconciling two records that never match.

And when something odd surfaces the week of a run, a new hire mid-period, a garnishment, a tip pool dispute, you get an answer the same business day. For a restaurant, payroll help that replies on Thursday is not help.

Kitchen, Front of House, and 1099s

Restaurants mix worker types more than almost any other business: W-2 staff in the kitchen and on the floor, and the occasional 1099 contractor, a musician, a one-off repair, a consulting chef. Paying someone as a contractor who works like an employee builds up back payroll taxes and penalties that surface at the worst time. We set each worker up correctly at hire, keep the W-9s and pay records straight, and issue clean W-2s and 1099s in January.

The filings run on the same rails as every payroll we process: Florida reemployment tax filed quarterly, federal deposits on your required schedule, and the quarterly Form 941 reconciled to what was actually withheld. The full breakdown is in our plain-English guide to Florida payroll taxes for small business owners.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you fix tip allocation mistakes from past payrolls?

Do you run weekly payroll for restaurants?

Do you handle Florida and federal payroll taxes for restaurants?
