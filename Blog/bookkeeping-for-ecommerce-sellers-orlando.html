Bookkeeping for Orlando E-Commerce Sellers

Ryan Shea • September 2026 • 8 min read • QuickBooks Level 2 ProAdvisor

Selling online generates a firehose of data: orders, refunds, platform fees, processor fees, payouts, and sales tax collected in a dozen states. Standard bookkeeping was not built for that shape, and it is why so many e-commerce owners have books that never quite tie out to their bank account.

Here is what makes e-commerce bookkeeping different, where it goes wrong, and what clean books look like for a Shopify or multi-channel seller in Orlando.

Why E-Commerce Books Are Different

The core issue is that your revenue never arrives whole. When you sell a hundred dollars of product on Shopify, the deposit that lands in your bank is smaller, because the platform and the payment processor take their cut first, and any refund or chargeback comes out before payout too. Record only what hit the bank and you understate both your sales and your fees, and your margins become fiction. Clean e-commerce books capture gross sales, then break out fees and refunds as their own line items.

Sales Tax and Economic Nexus

This is the piece that worries online sellers most, and for good reason. Most states now require an out-of-state seller to collect their sales tax once you cross an economic nexus threshold, typically a set dollar amount of sales or a number of transactions into that state per year. The thresholds and rules differ from one state to the next, so where you owe depends on where and how much you sell. Because it is specific to your situation, confirm your obligations rather than assume. What bookkeeping does is keep the sales-by-state records accurate and current, so that when you know where you have nexus, filing is a clean report instead of a year-end reconstruction. Shea Business Solutions offers sales tax filing support built on those records.

Inventory and Cost of Goods Sold

If you hold stock, your profit is only as accurate as your cost of goods sold. Recording purchases as a lump expense when you buy, rather than matching cost to the sales they produced, makes a good month look bad and a bad one look fine. Proper inventory and COGS tracking ties the cost of each product to the revenue it earned, which is also what a lender or a buyer will want to see. The same discipline applies to a brick-and-mortar shop, which we cover on our retail bookkeeping page.

Connecting Shopify to QuickBooks

Done right, your store and your books talk to each other. Shopify and the payment processors behind it can feed QuickBooks Online so orders, fees, and payouts flow in automatically and reconcile against your real bank deposits. Set up wrong, that same connection doubles up sales or buries fees, and you spend month end untangling it. Getting the mapping right at setup is the difference, which is why a proper QuickBooks setup pays for itself for an online seller.

The Bottom Line

E-commerce bookkeeping is a different job because the money arrives net, the sales span channels and states, and inventory sits between what you spent and what you earned. Books that record gross sales, separate the fees, track tax by state, and match cost to revenue give you numbers you can actually run the business on.

Quick Answers

Why is bookkeeping for an e-commerce business different?

E-commerce revenue arrives net, not gross. Shopify, Stripe, and PayPal deposit what is left after their fees, refunds, and chargebacks, so the money that hits your bank is smaller than what you actually sold. Clean e-commerce books record gross sales, then the fees and refunds separately, so your revenue and margins are real numbers instead of a bank balance.

Do I have to collect sales tax in other states?

Often, yes. Most states require an out-of-state seller to collect their sales tax once you pass an economic nexus threshold, usually a set amount of sales or number of transactions into that state, and the specifics vary state by state. Because it depends on where and how much you sell, confirm your obligations for your situation. Our role is keeping the sales-by-state records accurate so filing is straightforward once you know where you owe.

Can you connect Shopify to QuickBooks?

Yes. Shopify, and the payment processors behind it, can feed into QuickBooks Online so orders, fees, and payouts flow into your books instead of being typed in by hand. Set up correctly, it reconciles your platform sales to your actual bank deposits, which is the step most do-it-yourself e-commerce books get wrong.
