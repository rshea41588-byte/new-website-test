What Does a Bookkeeper Do?

Ryan Shea • September 2026 • 6 min read • QuickBooks Level 2 ProAdvisor

"Bookkeeper" is one of those job titles almost everyone recognizes and few can define. Most small business owners sense they probably need one, but they are fuzzy on what the work actually involves week to week, and where it stops. That gap is why some owners pay for help they never fully use, and others go years without books they can trust.

Here is the plain version: what a bookkeeper does, what falls outside the role, and how to tell when your business has reached the point of needing one.

What a Bookkeeper Actually Does

A bookkeeper records and organizes the money moving through your business so your financial records stay accurate and current. That sounds simple, and on the days it is done well, it is nearly invisible. The work behind it is steady and detailed.

The recurring tasks usually include:

  • Recording income and expenses as they happen, with the receipt or invoice filed behind each one
  • Categorizing every transaction so it lands in the right account
  • Reconciling your bank and credit card accounts against the statements each month
  • Tracking money customers owe you and bills you owe others
  • Running payroll, or preparing the numbers your payroll service needs
  • Producing monthly reports, usually a profit and loss statement and a balance sheet
  • Keeping the records complete so your tax preparer starts the year with clean numbers

Some bookkeepers handle all of this, others a slice of it. The scope depends on the size of your business and what you have chosen to keep in house.

The Monthly Bookkeeping Cycle

Most of a bookkeeper's work runs on a monthly rhythm. Transactions get recorded and categorized through the month, accounts get reconciled once the statements arrive, and the month closes with a set of reports you can actually read. Then it begins again.

The value sits in that closing step. Recording transactions is data entry. Reconciling and reporting is what turns the data into something you can make decisions from. A month that is recorded but never reconciled is only half done, and that half is usually where errors hide. If the reports still look like a foreign language, our guide to reading a P&L and balance sheet walks through the lines that matter.

What a Bookkeeper Does Not Do

This is where the confusion usually sits. A bookkeeper keeps your records accurate and current. An accountant, and especially a CPA, takes those records and interprets them: tax strategy, filing returns, representing you before the IRS, and higher-level planning. The roles overlap at the edges, and one firm may offer both, but they are not the same job. We break down the distinction in bookkeeper vs. accountant.

A bookkeeper also does not make the business decisions for you. The job is to hand you numbers you can trust, so the calls you make rest on what is real rather than what you hope is in the account.

What Clean, Current Books Get You

When the bookkeeping is done well, several things get easier:

  • You can see whether last month actually made money, rather than only watching the balance move up or down
  • Tax season stops being a scramble, because the year is already recorded and reconciled
  • A loan or line of credit application goes smoother, because lenders want current financials they can rely on
  • Problems surface early: a duplicate charge, a customer who never paid, a cost quietly creeping up

Clean books are less about compliance and more about not flying blind.

Do You Need a Bookkeeper?

A few signs point to yes. Your books are months behind and catching up keeps getting pushed off. Data entry is eating evenings you would rather spend elsewhere. A simple question like "what did I spend on materials last quarter" takes a lot of digging to answer. Or the reports exist, but you are not sure they are right.

If any of that sounds familiar, the fix is usually straightforward. Books that have fallen behind can be caught up, and once they are current, a monthly routine keeps them that way.

The Bottom Line

A bookkeeper records and organizes your finances so the books stay accurate, reconciled, and ready to use, month after month. It is quieter work than tax strategy, but it is the foundation everything else sits on. Without it, even a good accountant is working from guesses.

Quick Answers

What does a bookkeeper do day to day?

Day to day, a bookkeeper records income and expenses, files the receipt or invoice behind each transaction, and categorizes everything so it lands in the right account. On a monthly rhythm they reconcile your bank and credit card accounts against the statements and produce reports, usually a profit and loss statement and a balance sheet, so your records stay accurate and current.

Is a bookkeeper the same as an accountant?

No. A bookkeeper keeps your financial records accurate and current. An accountant, and especially a CPA, interprets those records for tax strategy, files your returns, and handles higher-level planning. The roles overlap at the edges and one firm may offer both, but the work is different.

Do I still need a bookkeeper if I use QuickBooks?

Software records what you tell it. It does not judge whether a transaction is categorized correctly or whether your accounts actually reconcile against the bank. A bookkeeper makes sure the numbers going in are right and the accounts match each month, which is what turns QuickBooks from a digital checkbook into reports you can trust.
