Hiring someone in Orlando to file your business return comes down to three checks you can make before anyone opens your books: whether the preparer holds a valid PTIN, what representation rights come with their credential, and whether the person filing the return is the person who knows how the year was recorded. The first two are defined by the IRS and are quick to verify. The third is where most small businesses actually get burned.

  • Anyone paid to prepare a federal return must hold a valid PTIN. No exceptions.
  • Attorneys, CPAs and enrolled agents can represent you before the IRS on any matter.
  • Other paid preparers have limited rights, or none at all.
  • A return built on unreconciled books files the errors along with everything else.

What Should You Check Before Hiring a Tax Preparer?

Start with the PTIN. The IRS is direct about this: anyone who prepares or assists in preparing federal tax returns for compensation must have a valid preparer tax identification number before preparing returns. A preparer who will not give you their PTIN, or who asks you to sign a return with the preparer section left blank, has told you everything you need to know.

The IRS publishes a searchable directory of preparers with credentials and select qualifications. It is worth two minutes before a first meeting.

A signed return is your return. You carry the liability for what is on it regardless of who typed it, which is why the preparer's credential and their access to your actual records both matter more than their software.

What Do PTIN, EA and CPA Actually Mean?

They describe different things. A PTIN is a registration number, not a credential. CPA and enrolled agent are credentials that carry rights with them. The practical difference shows up the day the IRS sends a letter.

Preparer typeRepresentation rights before the IRS
AttorneyUnlimited. Can represent you on any matter, including audits, collection and appeals.
Certified Public Accountant (CPA)Unlimited. Same scope as an attorney for IRS matters.
Enrolled Agent (EA)Unlimited. Same scope as an attorney or CPA for IRS matters.
Annual Filing Season Program participantLimited. The program requires 18 hours of continuing education, including a six-hour federal tax law refresher course with a test.
PTIN holder with no credentialNone. Since January 1, 2016 these preparers may prepare returns but have no authority to represent clients before the IRS.

Read that table as a map of what happens after filing, not a ranking of who prepares a better return. Plenty of accurate small business returns are prepared by people without unlimited representation rights, and plenty of businesses never need representation at all.

Who Can Represent You If the IRS Has Questions?

Attorneys, CPAs and enrolled agents, on any matter. That is the whole answer, and it is the single most useful thing to know before you choose.

If your business has open collection issues, an appeal in progress, or a genuine likelihood of examination, hire inside that group and do it early. If your situation is a clean annual filing off reconciled books, representation rights are insurance you are unlikely to draw on, and other factors deserve more weight.

Does the Highest Star Rating Mean the Right Fit?

Not on its own. A five-star average tells you clients were treated well; it does not tell you the preparer has handled your entity type or your industry.

Read what the reviews describe rather than counting them. A review that says a preparer caught a payroll misclassification, or rebuilt two years of records before filing, carries information. A review that says "great service" carries none. Volume matters at the margins, and a firm with four reviews and a decade of work is not automatically worse than one with two hundred.

What Does an Orlando Small Business Return Involve?

It depends on how the business is organized, and the entity decides which form gets filed.

  • Sole proprietor or single-member LLC: Schedule C filed with the owner's personal return.
  • Partnership or multi-member LLC: Form 1065, with a Schedule K-1 for each partner.
  • S corporation: Form 1120-S, with a K-1 per shareholder and officer compensation stated separately. Covered in detail on the S-corp tax preparation page.
  • C corporation: Form 1120, and the corporation is taxed in its own right.

Florida changes one part of this picture. There is no personal state income tax, so profit that flows through to a Florida resident on a K-1 or a Schedule C picks up federal tax but no state income tax on top. Payroll taxes still apply to wages, and businesses that collect sales tax still file with the state on their own schedule.

When Should You Hire a CPA or Enrolled Agent Instead?

When the work needs someone with unlimited representation rights, or an attest service that only a CPA firm can perform.

A few situations that point that direction: a bank or investor asking for audited or CPA-reviewed financial statements, an IRS examination already underway, unfiled back years with a balance owed, a business sale where the buyer's advisors will pick the numbers apart, or ownership structures spanning multiple states. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check that an Orlando tax preparer is legitimate?

Ask for their PTIN and look them up in the IRS directory of federal tax return preparers. Anyone who prepares federal returns for compensation must hold a valid preparer tax identification number, and the preparer should sign the return in the paid preparer section. A preparer who will not give you a PTIN or wants to leave that section blank is not one to hire.

Do I need a CPA to file my small business taxes in Florida?

No. A CPA licence is not required to prepare a business return, and many small business returns are prepared accurately by non-CPA preparers. What a CPA brings, along with attorneys and enrolled agents, is unlimited authority to represent you before the IRS on audits, collection and appeals. If representation is a live concern, hire inside that group.

What is the difference between an enrolled agent and a CPA for tax filing?

For IRS purposes the representation scope is the same: enrolled agents, CPAs and attorneys all have unlimited rights to represent clients before the IRS. The credentials differ in how they are earned and in what else the holder can do, since CPA firms also perform audits and other attest work that enrolled agents do not.

Should the same person handle my bookkeeping and my tax return?

It usually helps. When the preparer is also the person who reconciled the accounts through the year, the balance sheet, the owner draws and the payroll postings already agree before the return is started, and there is no handover where questions get lost. It is not required, and books kept elsewhere are reviewed first instead.
