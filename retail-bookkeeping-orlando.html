What retail bookkeeping actually covers

The monthly work on a retail file is the ordinary bookkeeping cycle plus three things a service business never deals with: stock, sales tax, and a point-of-sale system that has its own opinion about what happened.

  • Every bank and credit card account reconciled against its statement.
  • Point-of-sale deposits matched to bank deposits, including the card processor's fees and holdbacks.
  • Inventory purchases posted to inventory rather than straight to expense.
  • Cost of goods sold recognized when items sell, so margin is readable by month.
  • Sales tax collected tracked as a liability until it is remitted.
  • A profit and loss statement and balance sheet issued monthly, not reconstructed in March.

Why is inventory where retail books break?

Because inventory sits on the balance sheet and the P&L at the same time, and a mistake in one place quietly distorts the other.

The common version looks like this. Stock purchases get coded to an expense account when the invoice is paid, so the P&L shows a bad month whenever the shop restocks and a great month whenever it does not. Meanwhile the inventory asset on the balance sheet stops moving, because nothing is posting to it. Both statements are wrong, neither is obviously wrong, and the error compounds every month until someone counts the shelves.

If your gross margin swings by ten points month to month and the prices did not change, the problem is almost never pricing. It is when purchases and cost of goods sold are being recorded.

How does inventory valuation change your P&L?

It sets what each sale is deemed to have cost, which sets your gross margin. QuickBooks Online uses first-in, first-out for inventory items, so the oldest units are treated as the ones that sold. That is a reasonable default for most Orlando retailers, and it only produces sensible numbers when item quantities and costs were entered correctly at the start.

Getting the setup right matters more than the method. Items need real costs attached, receiving needs to be recorded when goods arrive rather than when the invoice clears, and physical counts need somewhere to land. A shop that counts twice a year and adjusts through a shrinkage account will read its own margins accurately. A shop that never counts is guessing, and so is anyone reading its statements.

A correction worth describing

One Orlando client, a custom manufacturer rather than a shop, had raw materials and cost of goods sold tracked so loosely in QuickBooks Online that the P&L no longer described the business. The work involved restructuring inventory tracking, linking job-costing, and setting proper allocations for materials and direct labor. It surfaced a $22,000 inventory valuation error and produced margin reporting the owner could use per production run.

The reason it belongs on a retail page is that the mechanism is identical. Anywhere goods are bought, held and sold, the same three failure points appear: what was received, what it cost, and when it left. A boutique with four hundred SKUs and a fabrication plant with raw steel have the same accounting problem at different scales.

What does Florida sales tax mean for retail books?

Florida's general state sales tax rate is 6%, and counties add a discretionary sales surtax on top that varies by county. For certain transactions, only the first $5,000 of a taxable sale is subject to the surtax. Rates and the counties that impose them are published by the Florida Department of Revenue and change from year to year, which is exactly why they belong in a table you check rather than a number you memorize.

For a retailer sitting near a county line, the surtax follows delivery into the county imposing it. A Windermere shop delivering into Osceola and an Orlando shop shipping to Seminole are not applying the same rate, and a point-of-sale system configured once and never revisited will get this wrong for years without complaining.

In the books, the piece that matters is treatment. Sales tax collected is money held for the state. It belongs in a liability account and it should clear when the return is filed. When collected tax lands in income instead, revenue is overstated, the liability disappears, and the business pays income tax on money that was never its own.

How do multi-channel retailers keep one set of books?

By reconciling each channel to its own deposits before anything is combined. A shop selling across a counter, a website and a marketplace has three settlement rhythms and three fee structures, and each one nets its fees differently before the money arrives.

The rule that keeps this clean: record gross sales and record fees as expense, rather than booking whatever the processor deposited. Otherwise revenue is understated by exactly the amount of the fees, and nobody can tell what the channel actually costs to run. Online-heavy sellers will find more detail on the e-commerce bookkeeping page, and shops with staff on a weekly cycle should read payroll services alongside this.

What does the monthly rhythm look like?

Reconciliations close the prior month, statements are issued, and anything unusual gets raised while it is still fixable. Sales tax filing runs on the schedule the state assigns the business, which for most Central Florida retailers is monthly. Year-end brings a physical count, an inventory adjustment, and financials that are ready for the return rather than a starting point for one.

Books that are already reconciled make the tax return a contained job. Books that are not turn it into a reconstruction, which is quoted separately as a catch-up bookkeeping project. Retailers who want both handled together should look at the bookkeeping and tax package, and general monthly service is described on the bookkeeping services page.

Frequently asked questions

How much does retail bookkeeping cost in Orlando?

Retail files are quoted as a flat monthly price agreed in writing before any work starts, so the fee does not move with hours. What sets the number is transaction volume, how many sales channels have to be reconciled, and whether inventory is already tracked properly in QuickBooks or needs to be set up first. Does QuickBooks Online handle retail inventory for an Orlando shop?

Yes. QuickBooks Online tracks inventory items and applies first-in, first-out costing, so the oldest units are treated as the ones that sold. It produces reliable margins only when items carry correct costs, receiving is recorded when goods actually arrive, and physical counts are entered as adjustments rather than ignored.

What sales tax rate does an Orlando retail business charge?

Florida's general state sales tax rate is 6%, and the county adds a discretionary sales surtax that varies by county. For certain transactions only the first $5,000 of a taxable sale is subject to the surtax. Because the counties and rates change, the current figures should be read from the Florida Department of Revenue rate table rather than assumed.

How often should an Orlando retail shop count inventory?

At minimum once a year, so the year-end balance sheet reflects what is actually on the shelf. Shops with high shrinkage or fast-moving stock are better served by cycle counting through the year, which catches problems while they are small instead of surfacing one large adjustment in December.

Can you clean up an Orlando retail QuickBooks file that is behind?

Yes. Catch-up and cleanup work is quoted as its own project after a free review of the file, separately from monthly bookkeeping. For retail files the usual work is re-posting stock purchases from expense accounts into inventory, rebuilding cost of goods sold so margins read correctly, and separating collected sales tax back out of income.
